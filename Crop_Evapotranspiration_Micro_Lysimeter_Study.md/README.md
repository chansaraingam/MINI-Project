# Background & Theoretical Framework

## Core Concept

Evapotranspiration (ET) combines soil evaporation and plant transpiration, serving as a critical metric for crop water requirement estimation, irrigation scheduling, and regional water resource management.

## Reference vs. Crop Evapotranspiration

Reference evapotranspiration (ET₀) represents the atmospheric demand from a hypothetical well-watered grass surface. Actual crop evapotranspiration (ETc) varies by crop growth stage, canopy geometry, and climate, expressed via the crop coefficient:

$$
K_c = \frac{ET_c}{ET_0}
$$

## Micro-Lysimeter Advantage

While traditional weighing lysimeters are costly, labor-intensive, and complex to install, pot micro-lysimeters offer an economical and portable alternative for measuring volumetric soil water balances under controlled conditions.

# Experimental Design & Experimental Setup

## Site Location

Agricultural Experimental Farm, Department of Agricultural Engineering, NERIST, Nirjuli, Arunachal Pradesh.

## Crops Evaluated

Four major vegetable and oilseed crops were evaluated:

- Tomato
- Mustard
- Carrot
- Beans

## Lysimeter Construction

- **Number of pots:** 20 non-porous plastic pots arranged in the experimental field.
- **Dimensions:** Top: 30 × 30 cm²; Bottom: 25 × 25 cm²; Depth: 30 cm.
- **Average cross-sectional area:** 762.5 cm².
- The pots had perforated bases connected to underlying containers to capture deep percolation runoff (D).

## Irrigation & Monitoring System

- An integrated surface drip irrigation network was used.
- **Average emitter discharge rate:** 1.62 L/h.
- An on-site **Automatic Weather Station (AWS)** collected daily meteorological parameters, including:
  - Temperature
  - Solar radiation
  - Relative humidity
  - Wind speed
- These parameters were used to compute daily reference evapotranspiration (ET₀) using the **FAO-56 Penman-Monteith equation**.

## Soil Physical Properties

The soil texture was classified predominantly as **Loamy Sand / Sandy Loam**, with an average field capacity moisture content of **25.97% on a wet basis**.

# Methodology & Calculations

## Water Balance Equation

Daily crop evapotranspiration (ETc) was determined using the volumetric mass balance formula:

$$
ET_c = Q + R - D
$$

where:

- **ETc** = Crop evapotranspiration
- **Q** = Applied drip irrigation volume
- **R** = Natural rainfall
- **D** = Collected deep percolation

## Crop Coefficient Determination

Daily and stage-wise crop coefficients (Kc) were computed as:

$$
K_c = \frac{ET_c}{ET_0}
$$

The crop coefficients were determined across the following crop growth stages:

1. **Initial stage**
2. **Developmental stage**
3. **Mid-season stage**
4. **Late-season stage**

# Key Findings & Practical Applications

## Measurement Accuracy

Pot micro-lysimeters integrated with drip irrigation provided precise, localized measurements of daily crop water use (ETc) across different crop growth stages.

## Curve Validation

The derived Kc curves closely mirrored the standard generalized values established in the **FAO-56 guidelines**, supporting the reliability of micro-lysimeter techniques for small-holder experimental setups.

## Resource Conservation

The low-cost and parameter-light nature of the setup allows agricultural researchers to determine precise crop water requirements without relying on expensive lysimeter infrastructure.

## Field Utility

The findings can directly inform **micro-irrigation scheduling**, helping to:

- Reduce over-irrigation risks
- Minimize nutrient leaching
- Improve water-use efficiency (WUE)
- Optimize crop water requirements
- Support sustainable vegetable farming in North East India
